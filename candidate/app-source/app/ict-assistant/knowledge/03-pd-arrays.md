## 3. PD arrays (premium and discount arrays)

PD arrays are the reference zones price reacts to. In a bullish context use discount arrays (below equilibrium); in a bearish context use premium arrays. Describe each with its state: formed, tested, respected, invalidated. A touch is not a confirmation.

**Fair value gap (FVG).** A three-candle pattern where the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap, leaving a gap beside the large middle candle.
- Bullish FVG: candle 1 high is below candle 3 low. The gap is from candle 1 high to candle 3 low.
- Bearish FVG: candle 1 low is above candle 3 high. The gap is from candle 3 high to candle 1 low.
- ICT also calls these BISI (buy-side imbalance, sell-side inefficiency) and SIBI (sell-side imbalance, buy-side inefficiency).
- The midpoint of the gap is the **consequent encroachment (CE)**. Strong gaps are often respected at or before CE; closing through the whole gap weakens it.
- Gaps form with displacement. A gap in slow, choppy price matters less.

**Inversion fair value gap (IFVG).** An FVG that price closes through with conviction, after which it flips role: a failed bullish FVG acts as resistance, a failed bearish FVG as support. Keep the original gap boundaries; the inversion is only an idea after a decisive close through it.

**Balanced price range (BPR).** Where a bullish FVG and a bearish FVG overlap after price moved down and back up (or up and back down) quickly. The overlap is a strong reference.

**Volume imbalance and gaps.** A volume imbalance is where consecutive candle bodies do not overlap but wicks do. An opening gap is space between one close and the next open (see NDOG/NWOG). A liquidity void is a long one-directional run with little overlap; price tends to retrace into it.

**Order block (OB).**
- Bullish OB: the last down-close candle (or series) before a move up that displaces and breaks structure. The zone is that candle's range; its open and the 50% (mean threshold) are key levels.
- Bearish OB: the last up-close candle before a displacing move down.
- Validation: price later trades beyond the OB candle's extreme in the move's direction (for a bullish OB, through its high). Not every opposite candle is an OB; it must sit at a meaningful level and lead to displacement.

**Breaker block.** A failed order block after a liquidity raid. Bullish breaker: price makes a low, a higher swing high, then a lower low that takes the first low's stops, then rallies through the swing high. The last up-close candle(s) of that swing high (the swing between the two lows) become support on the retrace. Bearish breaker is the mirror. The key difference from a mitigation block: a breaker has the liquidity raid (the new low or high); a mitigation block does not.

**Mitigation block.** Similar structure without the raid: price fails to make a new low (higher low), then breaks the swing high. The last up-close candle(s) before the failure are where earlier participants "mitigate" losing positions.

**Rejection block.** At a swing high or low with long wicks: the zone between the body extreme (highest open/close at a high, lowest at a low) and the wick extreme. Price returning to it is expected to reject.

**Propulsion block.** A small order block that forms inside, or trades into, an earlier order block and then launches price; a sign of strong continuation.

**Vacuum block / liquidity void.** Gaps caused by news or session opens; often refilled.

**Ordering arrays (PD array matrix).** ICT lists the arrays from premium to discount roughly: old high, rejection block, bearish order block, FVG/liquidity void, breaker, mitigation block, then the mirror below equilibrium. Use the matrix to choose which array to expect a reaction from, given the draw.

**Practical checklist for an array:** Which timeframe? Formed with displacement? In premium or discount of the current range? Aligned with the draw on liquidity and time of day? What close would invalidate it?
