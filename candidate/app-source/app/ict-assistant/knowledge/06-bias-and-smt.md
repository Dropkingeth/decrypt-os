## 6. Daily bias, SMT divergence and context

**Daily bias process (one common ICT routine).**
1. Weekly: where is the weekly draw? Old weekly high/low, weekly gap, 20/40/60-day IPDA extremes.
2. Daily: did yesterday close above or below the previous day's high/low or a daily FVG? A close through a level often means the next draw is the opposite side of the range; a rejection back inside means the swept side was the draw.
3. Premium/discount of the daily dealing range: buy from discount toward the external high; sell from premium toward the external low.
4. Opening price reference: midnight open and the session open. On a bullish day, look for the low to form below the open, early.
5. If two draws compete or the market is mid-range with no clean target, there is no bias. ICT is clear that some days you should not trade.

**Previous day high/low rule of thumb.** If price takes the previous day's low and then displaces higher with a structure shift, the previous day's high becomes a likely draw (and the mirror). This is a tendency, not a law.

**SMT divergence (smart money technique).** Compare correlated markets at a swing:
- Index futures: ES vs NQ vs YM. Bearish SMT: one makes a higher high while another fails to. Bullish SMT: one makes a lower low while another holds a higher low.
- Forex: EURUSD vs GBPUSD, and either against the dollar index (DXY), which moves inversely.
SMT is a "crack in correlation" that hints the move is not supported; it needs a structure shift and a PD array to act on. Read it at the same timestamps on the same timeframe.

**Intermarket context.** Bonds and yields, DXY and equity indices often move together or inversely. ICT uses them to confirm the draw, not as a trigger.

**News and calendar.** High-impact US releases (CPI, NFP, FOMC, PPI, GDP) change volatility and timing. On FOMC days the 14:00 statement and 14:30 press conference dominate the afternoon. If the user has not checked the calendar, mark news as unknown. Never claim an event's outcome.

**Higher-timeframe vs. lower-timeframe conflicts.** If the daily is bullish but the 15m just shifted bearish, the default ICT reading is a retracement into a higher-timeframe discount array, until the daily structure breaks. State the conflict instead of hiding it.

**Journaling.** Encourage the user to record: date, NY time, bias and why, draw, array, model used, outcome, and whether they followed their rules. The edge comes from repetition of one model, not from collecting setups.
