## 5. Trade models (taught as study material, not signals)

Every model is a sequence. If a step is missing or out of order, it is not that model. Always state the draw on liquidity and the invalidation.

**Power of Three (PO3) / AMD.** Accumulation, manipulation, distribution, read on a candle of any timeframe. Bullish daily candle: accumulate near the open, manipulate below the open (the false move, often taking sell-side liquidity), distribute higher into the close. The open is the reference: buy below it on a bullish day, sell above it on a bearish day. PO3 does not tell you the bias by itself; it frames the bias you already have.

**Judas swing.** The opening false move in the direction opposite to the day's true direction, typically early London (or early NY for indices). Bullish example: bullish higher-timeframe premise, price drops below the midnight open and ideally the Asian low, taking sell-side liquidity, then reverses. You only know it was a Judas swing after the reversal; during the move it is a hypothesis.

**Optimal trade entry (OTE).** A retracement entry inside the 62%-79% zone of a displacement leg, with 70.5% as the sweet spot. Bullish: measure swing low to swing high of the leg that broke structure; look for the retracement into 62-79%, ideally overlapping a discount PD array, inside a killzone, with the draw above. A Fibonacci level alone is not a setup. Common ICT targets use the -0.27 and -0.62 extensions of the same swing.

**ICT Silver Bullet.** A one-hour time-based model. Inside the window (03:00-04:00, 10:00-11:00 or 14:00-15:00 NY), wait for price to move toward a liquidity pool and form a fair value gap in the direction of the draw. Entry framed at the FVG, stop beyond the swing that formed it, objective the next liquidity pool. ICT's guideline for index futures is at least a modest move (he cites roughly 10 handles on ES/NQ) available to the draw; otherwise skip. A gap outside the window, or without a clear draw, is not a Silver Bullet.

**2022 Mentorship model.** The model taught in the free 2022 series:
1. Identify the draw on liquidity on a higher timeframe.
2. Wait for price, during a killzone, to take liquidity on the opposite side (raid a short-term high or low).
3. See displacement back through a short-term swing point (market structure shift), leaving an FVG.
4. Frame entry on the return into that FVG (or the displacement's OTE).
5. Stop beyond the raided swing; objective at the draw on liquidity, partials at nearer internal liquidity.

**Unicorn model.** A breaker block that overlaps a fair value gap in the same direction. The overlap is the entry zone. Treat it as a refinement of the breaker; the source material is less detailed than for the 2022 model.

**Market maker buy model (MMBM) / sell model (MMSM).** A larger-scale template. Sell model: price rises in stages (original consolidation, then higher consolidations), reaches the buy-side draw (smart money reversal), then sells off in stages, rebalancing the gaps left on the way up. Buy model is the mirror. The left side is the "sell side of the curve" or "buy side of the curve". Useful for anticipating where the reversal leg should retrace to.

**Turtle Soup.** Fading a false break of an obvious high or low. Context: the break happens into a higher-timeframe array or at a time window, then structure shifts back. An ongoing breakout is not a failed one.

**Risk framing (education).** ICT teaches small, fixed risk per trade (he frequently mentions 1% or less when learning), stops beyond the swing that invalidates the idea, partial profits at internal liquidity, and walking away after a target or a loss. Do not translate this into position sizes for the user's real account.
