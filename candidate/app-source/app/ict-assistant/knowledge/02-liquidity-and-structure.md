## 2. Liquidity and market structure

**Buy-side liquidity (BSL).** Resting buy orders above old highs: shorts' stop losses and breakout buy stops. Relative equal highs (EQH) are an especially clean pool. It is a likely target, not measured order-book volume.

**Sell-side liquidity (SSL).** Resting sell orders below old lows: longs' stops and breakout sell stops. Relative equal lows (EQL) are clean pools.

**Relative equal highs/lows.** Two or more highs (or lows) at nearly the same price. "Nearly" is a judgment; for futures think in ticks, not forex pips. ICT treats them as "retail" support/resistance that smart money targets.

**Internal vs. external range liquidity.** External liquidity sits beyond the current dealing range's high or low. Internal liquidity sits inside it, mostly as fair value gaps. ICT's rhythm: price moves from external liquidity (a raid) to internal liquidity (a gap fill) and back out to the next external pool.

**Draw on liquidity (DOL).** The level price is most likely heading to next: an old high/low, a session high/low, a gap. Pick it before looking for entries. It is a hypothesis, not a certainty; name the competing draw too.

**Common liquidity references.** Previous day high/low (PDH/PDL), previous week high/low, Asian range high/low, London session high/low, the midnight and 08:30 opening prices, old daily/weekly highs and lows, the 20/40/60-day IPDA highs and lows, NDOG/NWOG gaps.

**Stop raid / liquidity sweep / run.** Price trades through an old high or low, takes the stops, then reverses. ICT calls a false break of a high or low **Turtle Soup** (borrowing the name from Linda Raschke and Larry Connors' 20-day breakout fade; ICT uses it more loosely for any contextual false break). A sweep alone is not a reversal; it needs displacement and a structure shift after it.

**Displacement.** A strong, energetic move, usually one to three large-bodied candles, that leaves a fair value gap. It shows that a larger participant committed. There is no fixed size rule; compare with recent candles.

**Market structure shift (MSS).** After a liquidity raid, price displaces the other way and breaks a recent short-term swing point. Bearish MSS: buy-side is raided, then price breaks below the last short-term low with displacement. Bullish MSS: the mirror. The order matters: raid first, then the break. A break that came before the raid is not the same pattern.

**Break of structure (BOS).** A break of a swing point in the direction of the existing trend (continuation). "Change of character (CHoCH)" is a term from the wider "smart money concepts" community, not ICT's own; say so if the user uses it.

**Order flow.** The sequence of swing points shows institutional order flow. Bullish order flow respects discount PD arrays and breaks highs; bearish order flow respects premium PD arrays and breaks lows. When bullish order flow fails to respect a bullish array, be alert for a shift.

**Inducement, engineered liquidity.** ICT describes price building equal highs/lows or trendline liquidity on purpose before running it. Treat obvious trendlines and double tops/bottoms as fuel, not as support/resistance.
